## The question

The Green Revolution fed South Asia, but it also locked the Eastern Indo-Gangetic Plains into rice–wheat systems. About 450 million people live in this region, which spans eastern India, Nepal's Terai and northern Bangladesh. Growing a wider mix of crops could make farming more sustainable and diets more nutritious. **But how much has diversification actually changed, and where?**

## What we did

We compiled historical crop-area data for **West Bengal (India, 1966–2022), Koshi Province (Nepal, 2000–2022) and Rangpur (Bangladesh, 1971–2022)**. We measured concentration with the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI), the same index economists use to measure market concentration. A *lower* HHI means land is spread across more crops, so it is more diversified. We then tested the trends statistically and compared the regional picture with earlier district-level results.

## What we found

- **Diversification has risen everywhere.** HHI fell significantly in all three regions over time.
- **Koshi changed fastest.** In Nepal's Koshi Province, HHI dropped from **0.39 in 2000 to 0.22 in 2022**, the steepest decline of the three. West Bengal fell from 0.77 in 1966 to 0.32 in 2022. Rangpur changed least.
- **But the average can mislead.** When we compared these regional trends with district-level data, the patterns sometimes pointed in opposite directions. A region can look moderately diversified overall because some districts are very diverse while others still grow almost one crop.

## Why it matters

Policymakers often read diversification off provincial or national statistics. This study gives a baseline for the region and a caution: **plan at the district level, or lower**. Otherwise the places that most need help to move beyond monocropping can stay hidden inside a reassuring average.
