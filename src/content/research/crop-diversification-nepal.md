## The question

Rice, maize and wheat cover more than three-quarters of Nepal's cultivated land. Leaning this heavily on a few cereals leaves farmers exposed to bad weather and price swings, and leaves diets short of variety. For decades, nearly every national plan has said Nepal should diversify into vegetables, fruits, pulses and other crops.

So **has it happened, and did the policies actually help?**

## What we did

We combined two kinds of evidence:

- **A systematic review of policy.** We screened over 2,000 records from ministries, research bodies and donors, and analysed Nepal's periodic plans, sectoral policies and **23 diversification programmes** (11 government-led, 12 donor-supported).
- **Three decades of crop data.** Using the Simpson's Diversity Index, we measured how diversified Nepal's cropping actually was each year from 1990 to 2022, nationally and district by district. The index runs from 0 (one crop) towards 1 (many crops in balance).

## What we found

- **Real but modest progress.** National diversification rose from **0.39 in 1990 to about 0.48 in 2022**. It climbed through the 2000s as farmers took up vegetables, pulses and oilseeds, and peaked at **0.49 in 2015**.
- **Then it stalled.** Since 2015 the index has hovered around 0.48. That flattening coincided with federal restructuring, the 2015 earthquake and a new agricultural strategy. We are careful to say these happened *at the same time*; we cannot prove they *caused* the slowdown.
- **An uneven map.** Over the last decade, diversification grew in 43 districts but fell in 18. Gains have often stayed local.
- **Policy says yes; implementation struggles.** The policies consistently back diversification. What holds it back is weak market links, poor storage and cold chains, gaps in quality assurance, fragmented value chains, falling public investment, climate risk, and labour lost to migration.

## Why it matters

Nepal does not need another policy endorsing diversification. It needs the plumbing that makes diversified farming pay: markets, storage, cold chains and value chains, together with support that outlasts individual project cycles. This is, to our knowledge, the first study to link three decades of Nepal's policy history to measured diversification outcomes.
